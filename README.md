# OPERATIONAL EFFICIENCY ANALYSIS FOR MANUFACTURING INDUSTRY

## BACKGROUND AND PROJECT OVERVIEW 

This project aims to analyze the efficiency of manufacturing operations in four critical segments: production, quality, maintenance and supply chain. The analysis is performed on a synthetic dataset generated by artificial intelligence, inspired by real industry data from companies such as Siemens and GE. 

The project delivers a dashboard that synthesizes **11 North Star metrics** with **19 segment-specific visualizations**, built on a data-driven methodology and rigorous data quality validation. 

### Business Questions 

| Category | Primary Question | Key Metrics | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| **Production Performance** | How much capacity is being utilized? | OEE, Production efficiency, Throughput, Actual-Target Variance | 
| **Quality** | What is the performance of the quality system? | First Pass Yield, QC Pass Rate, Defect Rate | 
| **Downtime & Maintenance** | Why do breakdowns occur? | Maintenance need, Downtime Frequency, Avg. Downtime | 
| **Supply & Inventory** | How is supply chain efficiency? | Inventory Turnover, Avg. Lead Time, Warehouse Utilization, Product Trend | 
| **Sensor & Anomaly** | Are failures predictable? | Error Code Distribution, Sensor Temperature Dev. Index | 
| **Cost** | What is driving the cost? | Total Cost, Avg. Unit Cost, COPQ, Defect Cost

 ## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY  
Manufacturing operations demonstrate stable but suboptimal performance across most metrics, with a **critical quality crisis** requiring immediate intervention.

The production line demonstrated **consistent performance** throughout the year, generating **1.71 million units** of output with a controlled cost structure of **$99 million**. Maintaining a **unit cost of $55** confirms operational efficiency while the annual **COPQ of $2.1 million** indicates continued potential for improvement in quality processes. This clearly reveals areas requiring strategic intervention alongside strong operational discipline.

Operational performance was a period where the numbers show clearly: **OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) remaining at 68%** and **production efficiency at 70%** indicated areas for improvement of 17 and 15 points respectively compared to targets. **First-Pass Yield was at standard level with 98%**. However, **the inventory turnover rate remaining at 0.7** highlighted the need for optimization in inventory management. This presented a performance story where operational discipline and areas for improvement were balanced and clearly visible.

Critical performance indicators presented a clear picture of operational risks: a **39% Quality Pass rate**, falling short of a **40% Fail rate**, indicated a critical quality issue. **51% maintenance requirement and 34% downtime frequency** highlighted the need for high-priority intervention. However, the consistent **average lead time of 15 days** across all delivery methods suggested that process flow was still proceeding in a controlled manner at certain points. This picture summarized **an operational reality requiring urgent action in the areas of quality and maintenance.**

## INSIGHTS DEEP DIVE 
### 1. Output and Efficiency Trends 
Production efficiency remained constant at 70% throughout the period, 15 points below the industry benchmark of 85%, indicated a need for systematic performance improvement. 

Shift-based performance fluctuated within a narrow range of 69-71%, the evening shift had the lowest efficiency at 69%, highlighting the potential for improvement in operator and resource planning.

Distribution of OEE between 64-72% per machine with 20% of machines performing below average, supported the conclusion that the problem stemmed from planning and capacity utilization. This situation demonstrates that a consistent but low level of efficiency prevailed throughout the operation and the root cause should be made in systemic planning processes.

### 2. Quality Performance Analytics 
The quality overview clearly revealed a structural breakdown in the process: a pass rate remaining at 39%, a fail rate exceeding the success rate at 40% and a pending rate of 20% indicated a serious systemic breakdown in the quality control flow.

Defect rates of 2–3% per machine. Especially the fact that MAC-20, MAC-22, MAC-24, MAC-25 had a pass rate of only 42% showed that the problem was related to equipment calibration, maintenance or material flow not the operators. The rework rate remaining at a low level of 0.8% revealed that the capacity for recovering process errors was limited.

Total annual quality cost reaching $2.1 million, equivalent to approximately 2.1% of revenue, emphasized the need to address the financial impact of quality problems at a strategic level.

### 3. Technical Reliability and Sensor Diagnostics 
Maintenance data clearly revealed a structural challenge in equipment health: 51% of machines required maintenance and 34% of operations experienced downtime, coupled with an average of 5 minutes of consistent downtime, indicating a significant impact on operational continuity.

Positive picture was observed with very low deviations in temperature measurements from the machine sensor while the E01 error code was the most frequent problem, occurring in 40% of cases. Furthermore, the inability of the machine sensors to predict failures with near-zero correlation values ​​(vibration 0.01–0.04, temperature -0.01–-0.04) revealed the unreliability of the current sensor monitoring infrastructure. This situation indicates the need to redefine calibration thresholds in maintenance processes and to conduct comprehensive equipment inspections for mechanical or electrical failure modes that cannot be detected by the sensors.

 ### 4. Logistics and Warehouse Analytics 
Supply chain performance showed healthy portfolio diversification with product distribution evenly distributed among four products (25%) while the concentration of 70% of sales in Germany indicated a distinctly European-centric market.

Warehouse utilization showed Zone A having the highest occupancy rate at 40%, with other zones only at 20%, presenting an opportunity for consolidation to reduce operational costs.

Consistent lead time performance of 15–16 days across all transport methods confirmed process maturity while the inventory turnover rate of 0.66, well below the optimal range (4–10), revealed that stocks were held for longer than necessary and capital efficiency was decreasing. This situation highlighted the need for optimization in demand forecasting and production planning as a strategic priority.
